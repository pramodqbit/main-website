The Engineering Log / Brand concept

Software, on the record.

Qbitlog = qbit (the smallest unit of computing) + log (the habit of writing everything down). The site should read like a well-kept engineering notebook: every claim timestamped, every decision explained, every result measured.

The log above is illustrative: the entry numbers and week markers are invented, but the facts and metrics come from your Batra Hospital case study data.

§ 1The idea

Four principles the whole site obeys

A US or EU buyer choosing an offshore or nearshore partner is worried about three things: will they understand my problem, will they make good decisions without me, and will I see proof? The design language answers all three by showing its working.

Show the receipts

Every claim gets a number, a name or a screenshot.

“Fast, scalable solutions” gets cut. “3–5s per prescription at Batra Hospital” stays.

Decisions over decoration

Explain why, not just what.

Case studies are built around a decision log: options considered, what we picked, and why. This is what separates you from template agencies.

One accent, spent on meaning

Violet marks Qbitlog. Amber marks measured results.

No gradients, glows or glass. Everything else is ink on paper, so the two colours always mean something.

Calm motion

Things get written, they don’t bounce.

Lines appear the way a log is written, numbers settle into place, and there is no parallax or smooth-scroll hijacking.

§ 6Rules

Do and don’t

Do

  • Lead every page with proof: a client name, a number or a screenshot.
  • Use violet for brand and interaction, amber only for measured results.
  • Put metadata in mono labels (industry, duration, team size, date).
  • Annotate screenshots with lettered pins and a one-line “why”.
  • Write for a US/EU buyer: US English, time zones and overlap hours, compliance (GDPR, HIPAA) named plainly.
  • Keep one primary CTA per page: “Book a scoping call”.

Don’t

  • Purple→cyan gradients, glows, glassmorphism, orbiting icons, 3D globes.
  • Tech-logo walls as proof. Buyers care about outcomes, not your stack.
  • Unsourced numbers (“500+ happy clients”) or borrowed prestige (“Fortune 500 experts”).
  • Rounded pill cards with icons stamped on every block.
  • Smooth-scroll hijacking, parallax, or animation that delays reading.
  • Stock photos of handshakes and laptops. Use real team and product shots.
